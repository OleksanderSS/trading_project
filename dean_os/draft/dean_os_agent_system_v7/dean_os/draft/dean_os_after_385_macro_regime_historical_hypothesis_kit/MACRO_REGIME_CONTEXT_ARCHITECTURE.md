# Macro Regime Context Architecture

DEAN-OS should not treat a news item or law as isolated. The analyst must evaluate it inside the
current macro, credit, geopolitical, commodity, policy, and market regime.

## Why

The same policy can produce different outcomes depending on regime:

```text
low rates + fiscal stimulus → faster capex response
high rates + credit tightening → delayed or weaker capex response
war/sanctions/energy shock → state procurement and security-of-supply channels matter more
```

## Historical horizon

Use a long-run historical library from the late 1920s/pre-Great Depression through today:

- late 1920s / pre-crisis;
- Great Depression;
- WWII / wartime mobilization;
- postwar reconstruction and boom;
- 1970s oil shocks / Arab-Israeli wars;
- Volcker disinflation;
- 1990s globalization / tech boom;
- dot-com boom/bust;
- 2008 global financial crisis;
- post-2008 QE/low-rate regime;
- COVID;
- 2021–2023 inflation/rate shock;
- current multipolar / AI / energy transition / war-risk regime.

## Indicator layering

Older periods lack many modern indicators. Use:

Core long-run/proxy indicators:
- war/peace/major conflict;
- GDP or industrial production;
- inflation/deflation;
- interest rates / credit conditions;
- unemployment/labor stress;
- commodity/energy shocks;
- fiscal and trade policy;
- banking/financial stress;
- market valuation/risk appetite proxies.

Modern enriched indicators:
- PMI;
- credit spreads;
- yield curve;
- VIX/volatility;
- fund flows;
- earnings revisions;
- shipping/supply-chain indices;
- energy futures;
- sector factor returns;
- news intensity.

Rule: do not pretend modern data exists for old periods. Use proxies and preserve comparability warnings.

## v3: archetypes, baselines, and alignment

The analyst should not treat "1929", "1973", or "2008" as direct templates. It should tag mechanism
archetypes such as liquidity crisis, credit bubble burst, inflation spike, supply-chain shock, or
regulatory crackdown.

For event impact analysis, a perfectly clean control group is rarely available. Use cross-sectional
target/secondary/baseline baskets and explicitly label contamination risk.

For historical comparison, align frequencies carefully. Do not compare daily/intraday modern data
with monthly historical data as if they have the same precision.
